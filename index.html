About

I have spent fifteen years in the professional smart home industry — the CEDIA channel — on both sides of the counter. I started on the manufacturer side as employee number five at Ihiji, a remote-monitoring platform for integrators, then moved to OneVision Resources, where the product was the integrator's own client-experience and service operation. I then spent more than seven years at Bravas, one of the largest custom integration firms in the United States, working across operations, program management, partnerships, procurement, client experience and managed services as the company grew by acquisition.

I founded SignalPath Consulting Group, a boutique firm that brings enterprise-grade strategy and operations practice to the owner-led businesses that make up the custom integration channel. Most of those firms have grown past the point where instinct alone runs them well, and none of them has a strategy office. SignalPath is built to be that office on a fractional basis: strategic analysis and roadmapping, portfolio and go-to-market strategy, client-experience and managed-services programs, operational excellence, procurement and systems, and the market research that manufacturers and buying groups need to understand the channel they sell through.

I serve on CEDIA's Government Affairs Committee. My academic training is in political science and history, which is where the habit of asking what a number actually measures — before quoting it — comes from. This site is where I publish the analytical work behind the consulting: reproducible, documented, and honest about its limits.

Eighteen years of an industry top-100 ranking · R Markdown · Google Data Analytics Capstone

The custom integration channel's most-cited dataset is a voluntary, self-reported annual ranking of its top 100 firms. Manufacturers use it to size the channel and decide where dealer-program dollars go. This case study compiles all eighteen editions (2009–2026) into a documented, names-stripped dataset and runs three tests — coverage, composition and selection — to ask whether the list can support the decisions people make with it. The short answer: use it to find firms, not to size a market, compute a growth rate, or benchmark a dealer.
